Source: Summary of public WHO guidance and Indian consensus guidelines. Verify against the official documents before relying on it.

BMI (body mass index) is weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. WHO general categories are: below 18.5 underweight, 18.5 to 24.9 normal, 25 to 29.9 overweight, and 30 or above obese.

Guidelines for Asian populations, including Indian consensus guidelines, use lower cut-offs, with overweight starting at about a BMI of 23, because health risks rise at lower BMI in these groups.

BMI does not measure body fat directly and can be misleading for very muscular people. Waist measurement also helps assess health risk.

For a healthy weight, aim for gradual changes through balanced eating and regular activity rather than crash diets.
