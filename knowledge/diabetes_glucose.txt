Diabetes is a condition in which blood sugar (glucose) stays too high because the body does not make enough insulin or cannot use it well. Only a doctor can diagnose diabetes.

Common glucose tests: a fasting blood glucose below 100 mg/dL is considered normal, 100 to 125 mg/dL suggests prediabetes, and 126 mg/dL or higher on two separate tests suggests diabetes. An HbA1c of 5.7 to 6.4 percent suggests prediabetes, and 6.5 percent or higher suggests diabetes.

Risk factors for type 2 diabetes include being overweight, a family history of diabetes, low physical activity, increasing age and high blood pressure.

Lifestyle steps that help: eat a balanced diet with fewer sugary drinks and refined carbohydrates, aim for about 150 minutes of moderate physical activity per week, keep a healthy weight, and get regular health check-ups.

Warning signs include increased thirst, frequent urination, tiredness, blurred vision, slow healing of wounds and unexplained weight loss. See a doctor if you notice these. Do not start, stop or change any medicine without a doctor's advice.
